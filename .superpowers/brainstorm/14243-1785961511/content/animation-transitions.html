Animation behavior and transition design

Gameplay owns physics and outcomes. The animation controller selects one visual state from explicit priorities and lets committed reactions finish.

Committed sequences

Damage

Impact frame → recoil frame → recovery frame → apply shrink, checkpoint respawn, or defeat. Pit falls remain immediate and skip the animation.

Tail swipe

Wind-up → active sweep → impact → follow-through → recovery. The hitbox is active only during the middle two frames; repeated input cannot restart the sequence.

Transformation

Large-form hurt completes, then four shrink frames bridge into the small idle state without a scale pop.

Responsive locomotion

Ground

Idle, walk, and run use velocity thresholds with a short hysteresis band so frames do not flicker near boundaries.

Air

Jump rises through two frames, fall uses two frames, and landing plays only after meaningful airtime. Glide overrides ordinary air states.

Direction changes

A fast reversal triggers skid/turn; facing flips at the designated turn frame, never halfway through a sprite draw.

Defensive behavior

If a state or frame is missing, the manifest falls back to that form’s idle frame and reports a development warning. Atlas bounds, frame counts, baseline, and transparent margins are checked automatically before builds.
